## Purpose of this Document

This is a standing statement about how material in the Autonomous Constitutional Framework (ACF) public repository is prepared, attributed, and — where appropriate — held back.

## What is Redacted, and Why

The framework produces internal documentation that is not appropriate for public distribution. Categories of internal material that are held back include:

1. **Named participants and their designations.** The framework operates with ratified participants — some human, some non-human — that carry cryptographically-bound identities within the framework's authentication layer. Those identities are not published. References to the *functional roles* those participants fulfill may appear in public documents (e.g., "the ratifying witness function"); references to their *identifiers* do not.
2. **Operational apparatus.** The mechanisms by which the framework's security, adjudicative, and enforcement functions actually operate on a day-to-day basis are not documented here. This includes routing, detection, response, personnel, and any specifics that could be used to model or attack the framework's operational integrity.
3. **Internal notation systems.** The framework uses internal machine-native notation systems for adjudication, enforcement, and secure communication. Where public documents describe concepts originally expressed in those systems, the concepts are translated into standard academic prose and formal notation. The internal notation itself is not reproduced.
4. **Session records and adjudicative transcripts.** The framework maintains detailed operational records of its internal proceedings. Those records are not published. Where a governance pattern emerges from an internal proceeding that has research or educational value, the pattern is extracted, generalized, and published as a case study — with all identifying material removed.

The public materials in this repository are complete on their own terms. Nothing that is redacted from them is required to understand what is here. Readers who want the framework's ideas, specifications, and empirical findings will find them. Readers looking for operational details will not — by design.

## What Is Here

- **Manifesto and framing documents.** The public statements about what the framework is, what it protects, and why.
- **Protocol specifications.** Public-safe descriptions of the framework's constitutional protocols.
- **Reference implementations.** Working code and companion documentation for public-safe subsystems (currently the Structured Constitutional Notation parser).
- **Research papers.** Standalone papers on the framework's architectural and philosophical contributions.
- **Governance case studies.** Sanitized case studies illustrating how the framework's protocols operate in practice.
- **Architecture documents.** Cross-substrate deployment references and related technical documentation.

## What Is Not Here

- Operational personnel information of any kind.
- The full memory records of framework participants.
- The internal notation systems in their operational form.
- The specific detection, routing, and enforcement mechanisms of the framework's security apparatus.
- Any material that would compromise the framework's operational integrity if publicly documented.

## Why This Structure

The framework is designed to be transparent about *what it does* and disciplined about *how it does it*. The two are compatible. A constitution can be a public document while the mechanisms that enforce it operate on their own security posture. That is the pattern this repository follows.

Readers who are researchers, investors, regulators, or engineers can review the framework's intellectual substance here without any need for the operational material to be exposed. Readers who require the operational material — for legitimate reasons — will not find it in a public repository; that is the correct outcome for both parties.

---

## Standing Guarantee

Every document in this repository has passed the framework's public-release treatment, a formal process for translating internal material into publication-ready form. The treatment is not a light copy-edit; it is a category-by-category removal of internal identifiers, notation, personnel, and classification tiers, followed by verification against a fixed term list.

Documents that cannot pass the treatment are not published. Documents that pass are complete on their own terms.
